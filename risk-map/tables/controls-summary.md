| controlAuditRecordRepositoryIndependence     | Audit Record Repository Independence              | Ensure the durable record of what an AI system did is held under an authority the recorded workload does not control. The obligation attaches to the storage substrate (Audit Record Repository), not the components that write to it: an emitter can produce complete and accurate records and still not guarantee they survive an adversary holding administrative authority over the workload being recorded. That adversary is frequently the recorded workload itself, which is why a component cannot be its own audit authority.<br> Effective implementation provides three structural properties. **Separated append, read, and delete authority:** the authority to append records does not carry authority to read the trail back, amend it, or remove entries; audit read access is granted separately; and no principal holds append together with deletion or retention-shortening authority. **An administrative plane outside the recorded trust domain:** the authority to configure retention, grant and revoke access, and authorize deletion sits outside the trust domain of the workloads the store records, so compromise of a recorded workload confers none over the record of that compromise. **Self-recording of the administrative plane:** actions taken against the store — retention changes, access grants and revocations, deletions, expiry, and roster changes — are themselves written to the trail.
